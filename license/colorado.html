Colorado Real Estate License Exam Guide 2026

Step-by-step requirements, exam details, state-specific topics, study tips, and approved course providers for aspiring Colorado real estate agents.

Always verify current requirements with the CO Division of Real Estate (DORA/CREC). Requirements change.

Exam Note: Colorado has the largest state section of any exam on this list — 74 questions covering Colorado-specific law. Combined with 168 hours of pre-license education (second only to Texas), CO has among the highest entry requirements in the country.

Step-by-Step: Getting Your Colorado License

1
Complete 168 hours across 3 CREC-approved courses

Colorado requires 3 courses: Real Estate Closings (24 hrs), Contracts & Regulations (48 hrs), and Colorado Real Estate License Law (24 hrs) — plus 72 hours in principles-based courses. Specific breakdown varies by provider.

2
Apply to PSI and pay the exam fee

Register at psiexams.com and pay the $44.95 fee. Schedule at a PSI testing center in Colorado.

3
Pass the 154-question exam

Score 75% on BOTH the 80-question national section (60/80) AND the 74-question Colorado section (56/74). Both must be passed independently.

4
Submit E&O insurance and license application

Colorado requires proof of Errors & Omissions (E&O) insurance as part of the license application. Apply online at dre.colorado.gov. Pay the $200 fee.

5
Activate under an employing broker

New CO licensees are "Associate Brokers" and must work under an employing broker. Complete the broker affiliation through DORA's online licensing system.

Colorado Real Estate Exam — FAQ

Why does Colorado have 168 hours of pre-license education?
Colorado deliberately set high education standards to produce more knowledgeable agents. The state also defaults to "Transaction Broker" (rather than buyer/seller agent), which requires agents to understand a different agency model than most states.
What is a "Transaction Broker" in Colorado?
In Colorado, the default relationship is Transaction Broker — the agent assists both parties without representing either side as a fiduciary. This is different from most states where agents are typically buyer's or seller's agents. Understanding Transaction Brokerage is critical for the CO state exam.
What are Colorado water rights and why do they matter?
Colorado is a prior appropriation water rights state — water rights are separate from land rights and are bought and sold independently. For real estate agents, understanding water rights is essential and heavily tested on the CO state exam.
Does Colorado require E&O insurance?
Yes — Colorado is one of the few states that requires proof of Errors & Omissions (E&O) insurance before issuing a license. Your employing broker typically provides this through their brokerage policy.
Is the Colorado exam the hardest in the country?
It's consistently rated among the most challenging. With 74 state-specific questions (the most of any state exam) and a 75% threshold on each section, Colorado's exam requires thorough knowledge of CO-specific law, contracts, water rights, and agency. First-time pass rates hover around 45–55%.
