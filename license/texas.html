Texas Real Estate License Exam Guide 2026

Step-by-step requirements, exam details, state-specific topics, study tips, and approved course providers for aspiring Texas real estate agents.

Always verify current requirements with the Texas Real Estate Commission (TREC). Requirements change.

Exam Note: Texas has one of the highest pre-license hour requirements in the country — 180 hours across 6 required courses (30 hours each). The Pearson VUE exam has separate national and state sections, each with its own minimum passing score.

Step-by-Step: Getting Your Texas License

1
Complete 180 hours across 6 TREC-approved courses

Texas requires 6 courses × 30 hours each: Principles of Real Estate I & II, Law of Agency, Law of Contracts, Promulgated Contract Forms, and Real Estate Finance. You cannot skip any.

2
Apply for inactive sales agent license with TREC

Submit your application and fees to TREC before scheduling the exam. Pay the $185 license fee plus a $10 recovery fee. TREC processes and issues an approval to test.

3
Pass the background check

Texas requires a fingerprint-based background check through MorphoTrust/IDEMIA. Submit before or alongside your license application.

4
Pass the Pearson VUE exam

Score at least 56/85 on the national section AND 21/25 on the Texas section. Both must be passed — passing one but not the other means retaking only the failed section.

5
Sponsor with a licensed Texas broker

Activate your license by affiliating with a TREC-licensed broker. TREC requires a sponsoring broker to activate a sales agent license. Submit the activation online through TREC's system.

Texas Real Estate Exam — FAQ

Why does Texas require 180 hours?
Texas has a much stricter licensing requirement than most states to ensure agents are thoroughly prepared. The 180 hours must be spread across 6 specific subject areas — you can't substitute one course for another.
How long does it take to get a TX license from start to finish?
Most people complete the process in 4–6 months. The 180-hour coursework is the biggest time commitment. Online courses let you accelerate if you study full-time.
What if I only fail one section of the TX exam?
Texas allows you to retake only the failed section. You don't have to retake both national and state if you passed one. However, if you don't pass within 1 year of your first attempt, you must retake the full exam.
Does Texas have reciprocity with other states?
Texas does not have traditional reciprocity agreements. Out-of-state licensees must meet Texas education requirements, though some coursework may be waived for active licensees. Check TREC's current policy.
What is the Texas real estate exam pass rate?
The first-time pass rate in Texas is around 57–63%. The state section has a slightly lower pass rate than the national portion. Focus extra study time on TREC rules, intermediary agency, and promulgated contract forms.
