New Jersey Real Estate License Exam Guide 2026

Step-by-step requirements, exam details, state-specific topics, study tips, and approved course providers for aspiring New Jersey real estate agents.

Always verify current requirements with the NJ Real Estate Commission (NJREC). Requirements change.

At a Glance: New Jersey Requirements

Minimum Age18 years old

Exam Note: New Jersey has one of the lowest exam fees in the country ($15). The 110-question exam has separate national (80 Qs) and state (30 Qs) sections, each with a 70% minimum passing score.

Step-by-Step: Getting Your New Jersey License

1
Complete 75-hour NJREC-approved pre-license course

Take a 75-hour salesperson pre-license course covering real estate principles, NJ law, finance, contracts, and ethics from an NJREC-approved school.

2
Apply to PSI for the exam

Register for the exam through PSI Exams. Pay the $15 exam fee. Schedule at a PSI testing center in New Jersey or a neighboring state.

3
Pass the 110-question exam

Score 70% or higher on BOTH the 80-question national section and the 30-question NJ section. You must pass each independently.

4
Apply for NJ salesperson license

Submit your license application to the NJ Real Estate Commission. Pay the $160 license fee. Include your exam pass letter and school certificate.

5
Affiliate with a NJ licensed broker

Your application must include a broker's sponsorship. The broker signs your application verifying they will supervise your activities as a salesperson.

New Jersey Real Estate Exam — FAQ

Is NJ a good state to become a real estate agent?
Yes — New Jersey is a high-value market with median home prices well above $400K. Commission earnings can be substantial, especially in Bergen, Monmouth, and Morris counties.
Does NJ require a background check?
Yes. NJ requires you to disclose prior criminal history on your license application. The Real Estate Commission reviews disclosures on a case-by-case basis.
How long is the NJ pre-license course valid?
Your NJ course certificate is valid for 1 year. If you don't pass and apply within that window, you'll need to retake the course.
Can I get a NJ license if I'm licensed in NY?
Yes — NJ has reciprocity with New York (and several other states). NY licensees can apply for a NJ license without retaking the pre-license education, but must pass the NJ state portion of the exam.
What topics does the NJ state section cover?
The 30 NJ questions focus on the NJ License Act, Commission rules, agency disclosure (Consumer Information Statement), attorney review, and state-specific fair housing. This section trips up many candidates — study NJ law specifically.
