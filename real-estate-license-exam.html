5 Steps to Get Your Real Estate License

The process is similar across all states — here's the universal roadmap.

1
Complete Pre-License Education

Take the required hours of state-approved real estate courses. Ranges from 63 hours (Florida) to 180 hours (Texas). Topics include contracts, property law, finance, agency, and fair housing. Available online or in-person.

2
Pass a Background Check

Most states require a criminal background check via fingerprinting. Felony convictions may disqualify you depending on the state and nature of the crime. Submit your background check before or alongside your exam application.

3
Pass the State Licensing Exam

Sit for the state exam, administered by PSI Exams or Pearson VUE depending on your state. The exam has a national portion (covering real estate principles) and a state-specific portion (covering your state's laws). Passing scores range from 70–75%.

4
Apply for Your License

Submit your license application to your state's real estate commission along with proof of education, exam results, background check, and the license fee. Some states issue the license immediately; others take a few weeks.

5
Find a Sponsoring Broker

In most states, a new salesperson license must be held under a licensed broker. Interview multiple brokerages — consider commission splits (60/40 vs 80/20), training programs, culture, and market presence. Your first broker shapes your career.

Proven Study Strategies

Most people fail the exam not because the material is too hard, but because they study the wrong way. Here's what works.

Recommended Study Timeline
Weeks 1–4
Complete pre-license course + take notes
Weeks 5–6
Review flashcards, focus on weak areas
Weeks 7–8
Practice exams — aim for 80%+ before sitting
Final 3 Days
Light review only — rest and confidence building
Do 1,000+ practice questions

The exam tests how you apply concepts, not if you memorized them. Use prep sites like PrepAgent, Real Estate Exam Scholar, or Kaplan to drill hundreds of practice questions with explanations.

Learn real estate math cold

Math questions (commissions, prorations, LTV, amortization) are free points if you practice them. Many test-takers skip math — don't. A calculator is allowed on most state exams.

Master the vocabulary

Real estate has hundreds of specific terms. Use flashcard apps (Anki or Quizlet) for terms like encumbrance, escheat, emblements, hypothecation, and subordination. If you don't know the word, you can't answer the question.

Don't skip the state-specific portion

Many students study the national content and ignore state law. State questions are often harder and more obscure. Use your state's approved prep materials and focus on licensing law, agency rules, and disclosure requirements.

Score 80%+ on practice tests before booking

If you're scoring 65–70% on practice exams, you're not ready. The real exam is harder. Wait until you're consistently hitting 80%+ on timed practice exams under test conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to get a real estate license?
Most people complete the process in 2–6 months, depending on the state's required hours and how quickly you study. Online courses let you go at your own pace. States with more hours (TX: 180, CO: 168) naturally take longer than states with fewer (FL: 63, NY: 77).
How hard is the real estate exam?
The national pass rate is roughly 50–60% on the first attempt. It's not impossibly hard, but it requires serious study. People who just skim their course materials and hope for the best typically fail. Those who do 500–1,000 practice questions and score 80%+ on practice tests pass consistently.
What happens if I fail the exam?
You can retake it — most states allow multiple attempts. You'll pay the exam fee again (typically $40–$65). Some states require a waiting period between attempts. There's no limit on retakes in most states, but your course completion certificate usually has an expiration date (1–2 years).
How much does it cost to get a real estate license?
Total costs typically run $500–$1,500 depending on the state. This includes: pre-license course ($200–$600), exam fee ($15–$121), license application fee ($50–$245), background check ($40–$100), and NAR/MLS membership fees once you join a brokerage ($500–$1,500/year).
Can I get a real estate license with a criminal record?
It depends on the state and the nature of the crime. Most states evaluate applications on a case-by-case basis. Crimes involving fraud, dishonesty, or breach of trust are the most problematic. You can often request a preliminary determination from your state's real estate commission before investing in pre-license education.
Do I need a college degree to become a real estate agent?
No. No state requires a college degree to obtain a real estate salesperson license. Most states only require a high school diploma or GED. The pre-license course and state exam are the primary educational requirements.
Can I work in multiple states?
You need a separate license in each state where you transact real estate — but many states have reciprocity agreements that allow licensed agents from other states to apply without retaking the full exam. Check your target state's reciprocity rules.
