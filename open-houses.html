Open House Checklist: What to Look For

Use this checklist every time you tour a home to evaluate it systematically:

Exterior & Curb Appeal

  • Condition of roof (missing shingles, sagging, moss)
  • Foundation cracks or settling
  • Drainage — does water pool near the foundation?
  • Condition of gutters, siding, and windows
  • Driveway and walkway condition

Interior — Key Red Flags

  • Water stains on ceilings or walls (leaks, roof or plumbing issues)
  • Musty or moldy odors (especially in basement)
  • Uneven floors or sticking doors (settling or structural issues)
  • Age of HVAC system, water heater, and electrical panel
  • Adequate electrical outlets and panel capacity

Questions to Ask the Listing Agent

  • How long has the home been on market? Any price reductions?
  • Are there any known issues disclosed by the seller?
  • What are the average utility costs?
  • Why is the seller moving?
  • Have there been any offers?

Open House Etiquette & Strategy

Do: Bring your agent

Having Asad with you means you have expert eyes evaluating the home in real time — and it costs you nothing.

Do: Take notes & photos

After seeing multiple homes, they blend together. Document details about each property while you're there.

Don't: Disclose your budget

Don't tell the listing agent your maximum budget. That information can be used against you in negotiations.

Don't: Show too much enthusiasm

Even if you love the home, remain neutral in front of the listing agent. Save the excitement for after you leave.
