How Much Does It Cost to Sell a House in Albany, NY? (2026)

By Agent Asad Realtor  |  October 2026  |  7 min read

Selling a home in Albany, NY is exciting — but it's not free. Most Capital Region sellers are surprised by how many fees come out of the sale before they see their net proceeds. Understanding the full cost picture before you list helps you price strategically, plan your next move, and avoid any closing-day surprises.

On average, selling a home in Albany costs 8–10% of the sale price when you add up commissions, transfer taxes, attorney fees, and other closing costs. Here's exactly where every dollar goes.

1. Realtor Commission (Largest Cost: 5–6%)

The biggest expense is the real estate commission, which in New York State typically runs 5–6% of the sale price — split between the listing agent and the buyer's agent. On a $350,000 home in the Capital Region, that's $17,500–$21,000.

This commission covers everything your agent does: comparative market analysis, professional photography, MLS listing, showings, marketing, negotiation, contract management, and oversight through closing. Experienced agents more than earn their fee through better pricing, faster sales, and stronger negotiations — data consistently shows that agent-represented sellers net 17–26% more than FSBO sellers, even after commission.

Note on Buyer's Agent Commission (2024 NAR Settlement) Following the 2024 NAR settlement, buyer's agent compensation is now negotiated separately in a written buyer's agent agreement rather than being automatically offered in the MLS. Talk to your listing agent about how this affects your net proceeds and negotiation strategy in the current Albany market.

2. New York State Transfer Tax (0.4–0.65%)

New York State charges a transfer tax on real estate sales — sellers pay 0.4% of the sale price. On a $350,000 home, that's $1,400. If your sale price exceeds $1,000,000 (the "mansion tax" threshold), the buyer pays an additional 1%, but that does not apply to most Capital Region transactions.

Albany County does not charge an additional county-level transfer tax, but recording fees and deed stamps apply. Total estimated government fees: $1,500–$2,500 depending on the sale price.

3. Real Estate Attorney Fees ($1,000–$2,500)

New York State requires an attorney to represent each party in a real estate transaction — this is non-negotiable. Seller's attorney fees in the Albany area typically run $1,000–$2,500, depending on the complexity of the transaction, whether there are liens to clear, and the attorney's experience level. Your attorney reviews the purchase contract, prepares the deed, coordinates with the title company, handles proceeds disbursement at closing, and ensures all legal requirements are met.

4. Title Search and Settlement Fees ($500–$1,200)

The title company or closing attorney conducts a title search to confirm you have clean, marketable title to sell. As the seller, you typically pay for the title search ($300–$600) and any title-related fees. If liens, judgments, or encumbrances are found, those must be cleared from sale proceeds before you can close.

5. Pre-Listing Costs (Variable: $500–$5,000+)

Smart sellers invest before listing to maximize their sale price:

  • Pre-listing inspection: $400–$600 — catch issues buyers would use to negotiate, so you can price with confidence
  • Professional staging: $800–$3,000 — staged homes sell 73% faster and for 5–17% more according to NAR data
  • Touch-up repairs and painting: $500–$2,500 — fresh neutral paint returns $2 for every $1 spent
  • Professional photography: $200–$500 — usually included by your agent; confirm before listing
  • Landscaping: $300–$1,000 — first impressions set from the curb determine whether buyers even walk through the door

These are optional but high-ROI investments. Your agent can advise which ones pencil out in the current Albany market.

6. Mortgage Payoff and Prorations

At closing, your existing mortgage is paid off from the sale proceeds. This is not technically a "cost" — it's returning borrowed money — but it directly reduces what you walk away with. Your lender will provide a payoff quote (good for 30 days) that includes the principal balance plus any accrued interest to the closing date.

Additionally, you'll prorate property taxes, HOA dues, and prepaid utilities to the date of closing. If you've already paid taxes for a period the buyer will own the home, you'll receive a credit. If taxes are unpaid for a period you owned it, you'll owe the buyer a credit.

7. Capital Gains Tax (May Apply)

If the home is your primary residence and you've lived there for at least 2 of the last 5 years, federal law exempts up to $250,000 in capital gains (single filer) or $500,000 (married filing jointly). Most Capital Region homeowners will fall under this exemption, but if you've held the property as a rental or investment, consult a tax advisor before closing.

How to Maximize Your Net Proceeds

  • Price correctly from day one: Overpriced homes sit, go stale, and sell for less than homes priced strategically at market value from the start
  • Invest in high-ROI improvements: Paint, deep clean, and landscaping return far more than they cost
  • Choose an experienced negotiator: A skilled agent can more than make up their commission through stronger multiple-offer management
  • Time your sale: Spring and early fall are peak buyer seasons in the Capital Region — inventory is lower in winter, but so are buyers
  • Review all offers holistically: The highest offer isn't always the best — look at contingencies, financing strength, and closing flexibility
