Why Language Matters in Real Estate

Real estate transactions involve dozens of legally binding documents — purchase contracts, disclosure forms, mortgage applications, title documents, and closing statements. In New York State, where the law requires attorney review and both parties have independent legal representation, the terminology is specialized even for native English speakers.

For buyers and sellers whose first language is not English, the risks are compounded:

  • Misunderstanding a contingency clause can cost you your deposit or the deal
  • Missing a disclosure can lead to purchasing a home with hidden defects
  • Not understanding negotiation dynamics can mean paying more than you should — or selling for less
  • Confusion about timelines (inspection periods, mortgage commitment dates) can inadvertently trigger defaults

Common Concerns from Multilingual Clients

  • "I'm not a citizen — can I buy a home in New York?" Yes. Non-citizens, including those on work visas and green card holders, can purchase residential real estate in New York. Financing options may differ — some lenders offer ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number) mortgage programs.
  • "Will I face discrimination as a foreign-born buyer?" Discrimination based on national origin or immigration status is illegal in New York. If you experience it, you have legal remedies. Asad will advocate for your rights at every stage.
  • "I don't understand the mortgage paperwork." Asad walks every client through the key documents in plain language — in the language you prefer. You will never sign something you don't understand.
