Moving to Albany, NY — Everything You Need to Know (2026)

By Asad Butt, Realtor  |  September 2026  |  10 min read

Back to Blog

Albany, New York is having a moment. Once overlooked as "just the state capital," the Albany-Schenectady-Troy metropolitan area — known locally as the Capital Region — has emerged as one of the most attractive mid-size markets on the East Coast. Remote work migration from NYC, a booming healthcare and tech sector, three major universities, and home prices that are a fraction of downstate costs are driving a sustained influx of new residents.

As a realtor based in Latham, I help people relocate to the Capital Region every month. Here's my honest, on-the-ground guide to what moving to Albany is really like in 2026.

Where to Live: Albany Area Neighborhoods Guide

Most people who say they're "moving to Albany" actually end up in the surrounding towns — and that's completely normal. Albany is the hub, but Latham, Guilderland, Bethlehem, and Clifton Park have their own identities and often better schools. Here's a quick overview:

Latham / Town of Colonie

Median: $362,000

The most popular destination for relocating families. Excellent North Colonie schools (Shaker High School), proximity to Albany Airport, easy I-87 access, abundant shopping. A suburban feel without feeling remote. Best for: families, frequent travelers, state government employees.

Guilderland

Median: $382,000

Quieter, more residential than Colonie, with outstanding Guilderland Central School District schools. Larger lots, newer construction mixed with established neighborhoods. Best for: families prioritizing schools, buyers with slightly larger budgets.

Bethlehem

Median: $420,000

South of Albany, Bethlehem is affluent and suburban with top-rated schools. Excellent for families who want space, quality schools, and a slower pace. Longer commute to downtown Albany (20–25 min) but many residents find it worth it.

City of Albany

Median: $265,000

The most affordable option with the most urban character. Neighborhoods like Pine Hills, Center Square, and Helderberg are walkable and full of Victorian architecture. Best for: young professionals, SUNY/UAlbany employees, urban lifestyle seekers on a budget.

Troy

Median: $225,000

A rapidly gentrifying city with a thriving arts and restaurant scene, RPI presence, and some of the Capital Region's most beautiful brownstones and row houses. Prices are the most affordable in the metro for buyers willing to bet on continued revitalization.

Clifton Park / Saratoga County

Median: $410,000+

North of Albany along the Northway, Clifton Park offers newer construction, top-rated Shenendehowa schools, and a very safe, suburban community. Popular with tech workers and families. 25 minutes to Albany, 25 minutes to Saratoga Springs.

Jobs & Economy in Albany, NY

Albany's economy is far more diverse than its "government town" reputation suggests. Yes, the state government is the largest employer — but it's surrounded by a growing ecosystem of sectors:

  • State & Local Government: 100,000+ employees across state agencies, DOT, DOH, and legislative staff
  • Healthcare: Albany Medical Center, St. Peter's Health Partners, Ellis Medicine — collectively employing 20,000+
  • Higher Education: SUNY Albany, Albany Law, Albany College of Pharmacy, RPI (Troy), Union College
  • Technology & Semiconductor: GlobalFoundries (Malta) — a major chip fab plant — plus growing tech startups in the Albany NanoTech Complex
  • Finance & Insurance: KeyBank, M&T Bank, New York Life regional offices
  • Remote Work: A growing portion of Albany's population now works fully remote for NYC/Boston/DC companies while living at Capital Region prices

Things People Love About Albany

  • Four real seasons — fall foliage is spectacular (Catskills and Adirondacks are 1–2 hours away)
  • Weekend access — NYC 2.5–3 hrs, Boston 3 hrs, Montreal 3.5 hrs, skiing at Hunter/Gore Mountain under 2 hrs
  • Food scene — underrated restaurant culture; downtown Albany, Troy's River Street, and Schenectady's Little Italy
  • Albany Institute of History & Art, Times Union Center for concerts, Palace Theatre
  • Empire State Trail — 750 miles of connected multi-use trails crossing the state
  • Saratoga Springs — world-class racing, spas, dining, and summer festivals 30 minutes away

Things to Prepare For

  • Winters are real. Albany averages 60+ inches of snowfall annually. Budget for snow removal, good winter tires, and higher heating bills (natural gas is the norm).
  • Property taxes are high. New York State has some of the highest property taxes in the nation. Budget 2–2.5% of home value annually. The STAR exemption helps, but taxes are a real cost of ownership here.
  • The market moves fast. Good homes in desirable areas get multiple offers. Don't expect to "think about it for a few days" on a Latham colonial listing at $350K.
  • Car-dependent suburbs. Outside the City of Albany, most communities are car-dependent. Public transit exists but is limited compared to NYC.
Asad's relocation advice: If you're moving from out of state, plan at least one "scouting trip" to drive the neighborhoods you're considering. The Capital Region's towns are geographically compact but feel very different from one another. A day driving Latham, then Guilderland, then Troy will give you a much clearer picture than any website.
