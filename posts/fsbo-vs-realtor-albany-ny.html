FSBO vs. Realtor in Albany, NY: Which Gets You More Money?

By Agent Asad Realtor  |  October 2026  |  8 min read

Every year, thousands of New York homeowners consider selling their home without an agent — For Sale By Owner (FSBO) — hoping to save the commission. It's a tempting idea on paper. But in the Albany Capital Region market, the data tells a more complicated story. This guide gives you both sides, honestly.

What Does FSBO Actually Save You?

If you sell FSBO, you avoid paying a listing agent commission — typically 2.5–3% of the sale price. On a $350,000 Capital Region home, that's $8,750–$10,500 in your pocket. However, in most transactions you'll still be expected to offer compensation to the buyer's agent (currently 2–2.5%), and you'll still pay attorney fees, transfer taxes, and closing costs. So the real FSBO savings — compared to using an agent — is closer to $8,000–$10,000 on a typical Albany-area sale.

That's not nothing. But the question is: does going FSBO cost you more than it saves?

The FSBO Price Gap in Numbers

The National Association of Realtors consistently tracks sale prices for FSBO vs. agent-assisted homes. In recent years, the median FSBO sale price has been $110,000–$120,000 less than the median for agent-assisted sales. Even controlling for market differences, agent-sold homes consistently net 17–26% more — far exceeding the commission saved.

In the Albany market specifically, this gap matters because: (a) buyers who seek out FSBO listings often expect a bargain and lowball accordingly; (b) without MLS access, FSBO listings reach far fewer buyers; and (c) without negotiation experience, FSBO sellers often give up more in repair credits and contingency concessions during the inspection phase.

What a Realtor Actually Does

The common misconception is that agents simply list your home and collect a check. In reality, a listing agent in the Capital Region provides:

  • Comparative Market Analysis (CMA): Accurate pricing based on recent comparable sales — the single biggest factor in how much you net
  • Professional photography and staging advice: Listings with professional photos sell 32% faster
  • MLS exposure: Reaching 900+ buyer's agents and syndicating to Zillow, Realtor.com, Redfin, and 50+ other portals
  • Pre-qualification of buyers: Filtering out casual lookers from serious, pre-approved buyers
  • Offer negotiation and multiple-offer management: Maximizing your sale price when competing offers exist
  • Inspection negotiation: Pushing back on unreasonable repair demands — often saving $3,000–$8,000
  • Contract-to-close management: Coordinating attorneys, title, lenders, and inspectors so deals don't fall apart

Where FSBO Can Work

FSBO isn't always the wrong choice. It tends to work best when:

  • You already have a buyer lined up (family member, neighbor, coworker) — this removes the need for MLS marketing
  • You have a background in real estate, law, or contract negotiation
  • The market is extremely hot and multiple offers are virtually guaranteed with minimal marketing
  • Your home is uniquely desirable with little comparable inventory

Even in these cases, we recommend hiring a real estate attorney to review contracts and manage the closing — the legal complexity of a New York home sale is not something to navigate without professional oversight.

FSBO Challenges in Albany's Market

FSBO: The Advantages

  • Save listing agent commission (2.5–3%)
  • Full control over showings and timing
  • Direct relationship with the buyer
  • No agent communication delays

FSBO: The Challenges

  • No MLS access without a flat-fee service
  • Pricing risk (overpricing or underpricing)
  • Vetting buyers and proof of funds
  • Negotiating inspection credits alone
  • Legal and contractual complexity
  • Time: FSBO homes average 19 days longer on market
  • Lower pool of buyer's agents showing the home

Flat-Fee MLS: A Middle Path

If you want MLS exposure but prefer not to pay full commission, flat-fee MLS services list your home on the local MLS for a one-time fee ($300–$800). You still handle showings, negotiations, and paperwork, but you get broader exposure. You'll still typically offer a buyer's agent commission (2–2.5%) to incentivize agents to show your property. This approach is a reasonable middle ground if you're confident in your ability to negotiate and manage the process.

Bottom Line for Albany Sellers In a balanced or competitive market (like Albany's in 2026), the pricing expertise, MLS reach, negotiation skill, and deal management a seasoned listing agent provides typically generates more than enough additional sale proceeds to offset the commission — and removes months of stress from your life.

Questions to Ask Before Going FSBO

  1. Do I know what my home is worth — within 2% accuracy?
  2. Am I prepared to negotiate directly with buyers (and their agents)?
  3. Do I have time to field calls, schedule showings, and manage the transaction daily?
  4. Do I understand New York disclosure requirements and purchase contract law?
  5. Am I comfortable walking away from a deal if something goes wrong at inspection?

If you answered "no" to any of these, working with an experienced listing agent is almost certainly the better financial decision.
