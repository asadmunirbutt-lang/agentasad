The Home Buying Process in New York State: A Complete Step-by-Step Guide

By Agent Asad Realtor  |  September 2026  |  9 min read

Back to Blog

New York State has one of the most unique home buying processes in the country — including mandatory attorney review, a robust inspection and contingency system, and a closing process that typically takes 60–90 days. Here's every step, in order, with realistic timelines so you know exactly what to expect.

The Complete Timeline

1
4–8 weeks before searching

Build Your Financial Foundation

Review your credit report, pay down high-balance cards, and gather your financial documents. Target a credit score of 680+ for competitive mortgage rates. Calculate your debt-to-income ratio (target under 43%).

2
Before your first showing

Get Pre-Approved

Meet with 2–3 lenders to compare rates and pre-approval amounts. In NY, a pre-approval letter is expected with any offer. Choose your lender before you find the home — shopping lenders after you're under contract slows everything down.

3
Before you start searching

Hire a Buyer's Agent

Sign a buyer's representation agreement with your agent. In NY, the seller typically pays the buyer's agent commission, so this costs you nothing directly. Your agent schedules showings, runs comps, and negotiates on your behalf.

4
2–12 weeks

Search and Tour Properties

Work with your agent to identify homes matching your criteria. See homes in person when possible — photos often hide issues. Track your impressions and keep notes so you can compare clearly when it's time to decide.

5
1–3 days

Make an Offer

Your agent prepares an offer letter (not yet a binding contract in NY) including price, contingencies, and proposed closing date. The seller accepts, rejects, or counters. This stage is NOT legally binding — either party can still walk away.

6
3–7 business days

Attorney Review and Contract Signing

Your attorney reviews the purchase contract and negotiates any needed changes. Once both attorneys approve, you sign and deliver your 10% contract deposit. This is the moment the deal becomes legally binding.

7
7–14 days after contract

Home Inspection

A licensed inspector examines the property top to bottom. You receive a written report. Based on findings, you may request repairs, credits, or in serious cases, cancel the contract. Never skip this step.

8
30–45 days from contract

Mortgage Application and Appraisal

Submit your formal mortgage application. Your lender orders an appraisal to confirm the home's value. If the appraisal comes in below the purchase price, you have options — renegotiate, make up the difference in cash, or (with an appraisal contingency) cancel.

9
45–60 days from contract

Mortgage Commitment Letter

Your lender issues a commitment letter confirming you're approved for the loan. This satisfies your mortgage contingency. Avoid any major financial changes (new credit, job changes, large purchases) between this point and closing.

10
24–48 hours before closing

Final Walk-Through

Walk through the property one last time to verify agreed repairs are complete, all fixtures are still in place, and the condition matches your contract. If issues arise, your attorney can address them before closing.

11
Closing day

Closing

You sign all mortgage and transfer documents, pay your closing costs and remaining down payment, and receive the keys. Your attorney handles the legal transfer. Typical closing day takes 1–3 hours. Wire your funds in advance — NY closings typically require certified funds, not personal checks.

Closing Costs Budget: Plan for 2–4% of the purchase price in closing costs: attorney fees ($1,000–$2,500), title insurance ($600–$1,500), lender fees (1–2% of loan), recording fees, and pre-paid homeowner's insurance and property taxes.
