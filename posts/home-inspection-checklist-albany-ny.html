Home Inspection Checklist for Albany NY Buyers (2026)

By Asad Butt, Realtor  |  September 2026  |  10 min read

Back to Blog

A home inspection is the single most important step in protecting your investment during a home purchase. In the Capital Region of New York, where housing stock ranges from 1890s Victorians to 2024 new construction, what you're looking for varies dramatically depending on the home's age. After attending hundreds of inspections alongside my clients, I've put together this local guide to what matters most in Albany, Latham, Colonie, Troy, and surrounding communities.

Always attend your inspection in person. Walk through every room with the inspector, ask questions, and take photos. A written report is valuable, but nothing replaces hearing the inspector explain the severity of findings in real time.

Exterior — What to Check First

Roof (Critical)

Age of RoofAsphalt shingles last 20–25 years. Ask for age; inspect for curling, missing shingles, and granule loss in gutters. A roof needing replacement costs $12,000–$25,000 in Albany County.
Flashing Around Chimney, Skylights, VentsPoor flashing is the #1 cause of water intrusion in older Capital Region homes. Look for rust stains on interior ceilings directly below these features.
Gutters and DownspoutsCheck for proper slope, clogs, and that downspouts discharge water at least 6 feet away from the foundation. Improper drainage is a leading cause of basement moisture.

Foundation & Grading

Foundation CracksHairline cracks are normal. Horizontal cracks in block foundations or stair-step cracks in brick indicate structural movement and can cost $5,000–$30,000+ to repair.
Soil GradingGround should slope away from the house on all sides. Flat or negative grading directs water toward the foundation — the #1 cause of Capital Region basement water problems.

Capital Region-Specific Red Flags

Albany County and the Capital Region have unique issues based on local geology, housing age, and climate. These deserve extra attention:

Basement Moisture (Very Common in the Region)

Capital Region soil is heavy with clay and the region gets significant snowmelt in March–April. Basement water infiltration is extremely common in homes built before 1985. Look for efflorescence (white mineral deposits), water stains at the base of walls, sump pump pits (and ask when it was last serviced), and musty smells. Budget $3,000–$15,000 for waterproofing if needed.

Lead Paint (Homes Built Before 1978)

A significant portion of Albany County's housing stock predates 1978 — especially in the City of Albany, Troy, and Schenectady. Lead paint disclosure is legally required in NY, but inspection doesn't automatically include lead testing. Request a separate lead paint inspection ($300–$500) for pre-1978 homes, especially if you have young children.

Knob-and-Tube or Aluminum Wiring

Homes built before 1940 (common in Albany's Pine Hills, Center Square, and Troy's downtown neighborhoods) may have knob-and-tube wiring that hasn't been updated. Insurance companies often refuse to write policies on homes with active K&T wiring, and full rewiring can cost $15,000–$30,000. Have an electrician inspect separately from the home inspector if K&T is suspected.

Galvanized Steel Water Supply Pipes

Galvanized pipes were standard in homes built 1900–1960. They corrode from the inside, reducing water pressure and eventually failing. Look for orange/brown water, low pressure at upper floors, and visible corrosion on pipes in the basement. Full replacement typically runs $8,000–$15,000.

Oil Tanks (Underground and Aboveground)

Many older Capital Region homes heated with oil have — or had — underground storage tanks (USTs). A decommissioned or leaking UST creates an environmental liability that can cost $5,000–$50,000+ to remediate and can make a property nearly impossible to sell or finance. Always ask about heating fuel history and request documentation of any tank removal.

Interior Inspection Checklist

HVAC Systems

Furnace / Boiler Age and ConditionGas furnaces last 15–20 years; boilers 20–30 years. A cracked heat exchanger is a carbon monoxide risk. Ask for last service date and run all zones during inspection.
Central Air / AC UnitRun the AC during the inspection even in fall — inspectors often skip this. Systems over 15 years old should be budgeted for replacement ($4,000–$8,000 for a new system).
Ductwork ConditionCheck for disconnected ducts in the attic and basement. Poor ductwork wastes 20–30% of heating energy in older Capital Region homes.

Electrical Panel

Panel Brand and AgeFederal Pacific Electric (FPE) and Zinsco panels have known safety defects. If you see these brands, budget for immediate panel replacement ($2,500–$4,000).
CapacityModern homes typically need 200-amp service. Many older Albany County homes still have 100-amp or even 60-amp service — insufficient for today's appliance loads. Upgrade costs $2,000–$4,000.

Plumbing

Water Heater AgeStandard tank water heaters last 10–12 years. Replacement costs $800–$1,500. Ask for age from the serial number (inspectors can decode this).
Drain Test All FixturesRun all sinks, showers, and toilets simultaneously to check for slow drains indicating clogs or undersized drain pipes.

How to Use Inspection Results in Negotiations

In New York State, the home inspection contingency in your contract gives you the right to negotiate repairs or credits — or walk away with your earnest money back — if the inspection reveals significant defects. Here's how I advise my buyers:

  • Safety issues first: CO/smoke detectors, electrical hazards, gas leaks — these must be addressed. Sellers almost always fix safety items.
  • Big-ticket items: Roof, HVAC, foundation — negotiate a credit rather than asking the seller to manage repairs. You'll often get better work done yourself after closing.
  • Cosmetic items: Generally don't negotiate these. In a seller's market, asking for paint touch-ups or minor cosmetic repairs can kill a deal.
  • Walk-away items: Active structural movement, major water intrusion without disclosure, underground oil tank issues — these can justify walking away depending on price and your risk tolerance.
Asad's Advice: In the current Capital Region market, I recommend budgeting 1–2% of purchase price for repairs discovered during inspection, no matter how good the home looks. On a $340,000 home, that's $3,400–$6,800 in reserves. Homes that look perfectly maintained often have aging mechanicals that need attention within 1–3 years of purchase.
