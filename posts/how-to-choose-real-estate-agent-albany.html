How to Choose a Real Estate Agent in Albany, NY (2026)

By Asad Butt, Realtor  |  September 2026  |  8 min read

Back to Blog

There are approximately 3,500 licensed real estate agents in Albany County and the surrounding Capital Region. They range from part-timers who close 2–3 deals a year to full-time specialists who sell 50+ homes annually. In a market where homes sell in days and contract negotiation can make or break a $30,000 difference, choosing the right agent is one of the most important decisions you'll make in the buying or selling process.

This guide cuts through the noise and tells you what actually matters.

What to Look for in a Buyer's Agent

1. Local Market Knowledge — Specifically, Your Neighborhoods

Not all "Albany area" agents are equal. An agent who primarily works in Saratoga County may not know that the left side of a particular Latham street falls in a different school district than the right side, or that a certain subdivision has an active HOA with upcoming special assessments. Ask specifically: "What streets did you close deals on in the past year in [your target area]?"

2. Transaction Volume in Current Market

An agent who closed 20+ transactions in the past 12 months is actively navigating today's market — dealing with multiple-offer situations, inspection negotiations, and appraisal challenges regularly. An agent doing 3–4 deals a year may have gaps in current experience. Ask for their list of closed transactions; a good agent will readily share it.

3. Availability and Responsiveness

In a market where desirable homes go under contract in 3–7 days, an agent who can't schedule a showing until "sometime next week" is a liability. Your agent should be reachable by text/call within a few hours during business hours, and should be willing to show homes in evenings and weekends when you're available.

4. Negotiation Track Record

Ask to see their sale-to-list price ratio for buyer clients. An agent who regularly gets buyers into homes at or below asking (in a seller's market, this means close to list, not wildly over) is skilled at competitive offer strategy. An agent whose buyers are consistently 5–10% over ask may be winning deals by overpaying.

5. Lender and Inspector Network

Great agents come with a team. They have 2–3 lenders they trust who do SONYMA financing, get appraisals done quickly, and communicate clearly. They have inspectors who are thorough, available on short notice, and give actionable reports. A solo agent with no network is a red flag.

Questions to Ask Every Agent You Interview

"How many homes did you close in Albany County in the last 12 months?"

Look for 15+ for an active buyer's agent. Full-service agents with 30+ are typically strong. Part-timers with under 8 may lack current market fluency.

"Can you show me three recent transactions where you helped a buyer compete in a multiple-offer situation?"

This tests both activity level and strategy. A good agent can walk you through exactly what they advised — escalation clauses, waived contingencies, personal letters, timing.

"What's your process when a new listing that matches my criteria hits the market?"

You want to hear: same-day notification, quick showing scheduling (often within 24 hours), and a clear offer strategy discussion. "I'll send you the MLS link" is not enough.

"Do you work with any buyers who qualify for SONYMA or other down payment assistance programs?"

If they look blank, they're not the right agent for first-time buyers or buyers using assistance programs. DPA transactions have unique timelines and requirements that an inexperienced agent can mismanage.

"What are your fees, and do I pay them or does the seller?"

Since the 2024 NAR settlement changes, buyer-agent compensation must be negotiated separately. Most sellers still offer to cover buyer-agent commission, but you should understand the arrangement clearly before signing a buyer representation agreement.

Red Flags to Watch For

They pressure you to waive inspections on every offer

In a competitive market, some concessions make sense. But an agent who reflexively advises waiving the home inspection on every home isn't protecting your interests — they're just trying to win deals.

They talk about "exclusive listings" they have access to

All licensed agents have MLS access to the same listings. Anyone claiming secret exclusive access to unlisted inventory is usually misleading you.

They can't answer basic neighborhood questions

If they can't tell you the school district for a specific street, whether a neighborhood floods, or which side of a town line a property is on, they don't know the market.

They're also representing the seller on the property you're interested in

Dual agency is legal in New York but problematic — the agent cannot fully advocate for both sides. Unless you're comfortable with this, work with an agent who represents only you.

Green Flags — Signs of a Strong Agent

They push back on your assumptions when they're wrong

A good agent will tell you when your price expectation for a neighborhood is unrealistic, or when a home has a feature that will hurt resale. You want honesty, not agreement.

They know the inspectors, neighbors, and history of the areas you're looking in

Agents who have sold 10+ homes in a subdivision know things that aren't on the MLS — pending road projects, HOA issues, drainage patterns.

They set realistic expectations upfront

"In this price range in Latham, here's what you can expect: multiple offers, 5–7 days on market, homes going at 102% of list" — that's an agent who understands the current market.

One more thing: Chemistry matters. You'll be working closely with this person through one of the largest financial decisions of your life. If they don't listen well, can't explain things clearly, or make you feel rushed, trust that instinct and keep looking.
