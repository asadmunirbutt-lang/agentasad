Know Your Rights as a Home Buyer in New York State

By Agent Asad Realtor  |  September 2026  |  8 min read

Back to Blog

Buying a home in New York is a significant financial and legal commitment. The state has some of the strongest consumer protections for buyers in the country — but only if you know what you're entitled to. This guide breaks down your core legal rights as a buyer from offer to closing.

Your Right to Full Disclosure

New York's Property Condition Disclosure Act (PCDA) requires sellers to provide buyers with a written disclosure statement describing the property's known physical condition. This covers structural issues, water damage, pest infestations, environmental hazards, and more. Sellers who fail to provide this form must credit the buyer $500 at closing — but that credit doesn't replace the value of actual disclosure. Always request the PCDA form and review it carefully.

Disclosure Covers

Roof condition, foundation cracks, flooding history, HVAC systems, plumbing, electrical, mold, asbestos, lead paint (homes built pre-1978), pest damage, and any known neighborhood nuisances.

Your Right to an Independent Home Inspection

You have the right to hire a licensed home inspector of your choosing before finalizing a purchase. Never waive this right — not even in a competitive market. The inspection contingency in your contract gives you the legal right to:

  • Walk away from the deal if major defects are discovered
  • Negotiate a price reduction or repair credits
  • Request that specific repairs be completed before closing

In New York State, home inspectors must be licensed by the Department of State.

Your Right to Legal Representation

Unlike most states, New York requires both buyers and sellers to have their own attorneys present at closing. Your attorney reviews the contract, performs title searches, ensures all conditions are met, and represents your interests throughout the transaction. This is not optional — it is a legal requirement, and your protection.

Your Right to Fair Housing Protections

The New York State Human Rights Law extends beyond federal Fair Housing Act protections. In New York, it is illegal for sellers, agents, lenders, or landlords to discriminate based on:

  • Race, color, national origin, religion, sex
  • Disability or familial status
  • Age, sexual orientation, gender identity
  • Military status and lawful source of income
  • Immigration status (a New York-specific protection)
If You Experience Discrimination: File a complaint with the NYS Division of Human Rights (dhr.ny.gov) or the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). You may also have a civil lawsuit claim.

Your Right to Review and Negotiate the Contract

In New York, real estate contracts are not binding until both attorneys have reviewed and approved them. This "attorney approval" period — typically 5–7 business days after an offer is accepted — is your window to negotiate terms, request changes, or walk away without penalty. Use it fully.

Your Right to a Final Walk-Through

You are legally entitled to a final walk-through of the property within 48 hours of closing. Use this time to verify that agreed-upon repairs have been completed, that no fixtures or appliances have been removed, and that the property is in the condition described in your contract.

Your Right to a Clear Title

Before you can take ownership, the seller must transfer clear title — meaning the property is free of liens, back taxes, undisclosed mortgages, or legal disputes. A title search and title insurance protect you from hidden claims against the property. In New York, your lender will require lender's title insurance; you should also purchase owner's title insurance for yourself.

Common Title Issues to Watch

Unpaid property taxes, contractor liens, estate disputes, fraudulent deeds, boundary disputes, and undisclosed heirs. Title insurance protects you from all of these after closing.

Your Right to Back Out Under Certain Conditions

Your contract includes contingencies that protect your ability to exit without losing your deposit. Common contingencies include:

  • Inspection contingency: Exit if the home has serious defects
  • Mortgage contingency: Exit if you cannot secure financing
  • Appraisal contingency: Renegotiate or exit if the home appraises below the purchase price
