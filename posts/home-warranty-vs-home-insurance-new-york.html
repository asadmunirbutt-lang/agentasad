Home Warranty vs. Home Insurance in New York: What Every Buyer Needs to Know

By Agent Asad Realtor  |  October 2026  |  6 min read

Many first-time buyers in Albany confuse home warranties and homeowners insurance — or assume they cover the same things. They don't. Understanding the difference can save you thousands in unexpected repair costs and help you negotiate a better deal when buying a home in the Capital Region.

Homeowners Insurance: Protecting Against Disasters

Homeowners insurance is not optional — your mortgage lender requires it. It protects your home against sudden, unexpected events and covers:

  • Dwelling coverage: Repairs or rebuilds your home if damaged by fire, windstorm, hail, lightning, or other covered perils
  • Personal property: Replaces belongings stolen or damaged (furniture, electronics, clothing)
  • Liability: Covers legal costs if someone is injured on your property
  • Additional living expenses: Hotel and meals if you can't live in your home during repairs

What homeowners insurance does NOT cover: wear and tear, mechanical breakdowns, plumbing issues from aging pipes, HVAC failures, or appliance malfunctions. If your furnace breaks down on January 15th — one of the coldest nights in Albany — standard homeowners insurance won't help you. That's where a home warranty steps in.

Home Warranty: Protecting Against Breakdowns

A home warranty is a service contract — typically 1 year, renewable annually — that covers repair or replacement of major home systems and appliances that break down due to normal wear and tear. Coverage typically includes:

Systems Plans Cover

  • Heating system (furnace, boiler)
  • Central air conditioning
  • Electrical system
  • Plumbing system
  • Water heater
  • Ductwork

Appliance Plans Cover

  • Refrigerator
  • Dishwasher
  • Oven / range / cooktop
  • Washer and dryer
  • Garbage disposal
  • Garage door opener

When a covered item breaks, you call the warranty company. They dispatch a service technician. You pay a service fee ($75–$150 per call) and the warranty company covers the repair or replacement, up to the plan's limit. This is especially valuable in the first year of homeownership when you don't know the full history of the home's systems.

Cost Comparison in New York

  • Homeowners insurance: $900–$1,500/year in the Albany area, depending on home size, age, and coverage amount. Required for any mortgaged property.
  • Home warranty: $450–$750/year for a comprehensive systems + appliances plan. Optional but often negotiated into the purchase contract.
  • Service call fee per claim: $75–$150 (home warranty only)
Albany Reality: Old Homes Need Both Much of the Albany Capital Region's housing stock was built in the 1950s–1980s. Older furnaces, aging plumbing, and 20-year-old appliances are common. In these homes, a home warranty can pay for itself with a single furnace repair ($400–$1,200) or water heater replacement ($800–$1,500).

What Home Warranties Don't Cover

Read the fine print before assuming a home warranty covers everything. Common exclusions include:

  • Pre-existing conditions (items that were already broken before closing)
  • Cosmetic damage or items broken from misuse
  • Structural components (roof, foundation, walls)
  • Code upgrades required during repairs
  • Items not maintained properly (furnace filters, water heater flushes)
  • Secondary damage caused by a covered failure (if a leaking pipe damages your hardwood floors, the floor repair isn't covered)

This is why reading the contract before purchase matters — coverage limits, exclusions, and caps on replacement costs vary significantly by provider.

How to Use a Home Warranty as a Negotiating Tool

In many Albany-area transactions, buyers negotiate a home warranty from the seller as a concession — especially after a home inspection reveals aging systems. This is a smart strategy:

  • Instead of asking the seller to replace the aging HVAC (which they'll price low and may do poorly), ask them to provide a 1-year home warranty instead
  • A $500 home warranty is cheaper for the seller to accept than a $3,000 repair credit but gives you comparable protection
  • It also signals good faith — buyers who ask for warranties rather than repair credits often have smoother closings

Do You Need Both?

Yes — for most Albany-area buyers purchasing an existing home, the answer is you should have both. They complement each other with zero overlap:

  • Homeowners insurance = protection from disasters (fire, storm, theft, liability)
  • Home warranty = protection from wear and tear (mechanical breakdowns)

Together, they cover virtually every major financial risk a homeowner faces in the first year. The combined cost of $1,400–$2,200/year is a small price for that peace of mind — especially on older Capital Region homes with aging infrastructure.

When a Home Warranty Might Not Be Worth It

Skip the home warranty if: (a) you're buying new construction — new homes typically come with a builder's warranty covering systems and appliances for 1–10 years; (b) every major system in the home was recently replaced (new HVAC, new water heater, new appliances all under 5 years old); or (c) you have substantial cash reserves and prefer to self-insure minor repairs.
